# Scenario Finder and What Each Note Must Contain

## What is happening now

- A test to order, to skip or to leave for later: *The Test You Order and the Test You Don't*
- A critical value, an incidental finding, a result after the patient has left: *Closing the Loop on the Abnormal Result*
- A delayed scan, a bed or transfer that does not open: *The Delayed Scan and the Bed That Never Opens*
- A consultant who does not call back or come: *The Consultant Who Never Comes*
- A prescription, above all for a controlled substance: *Why This Drug, This Dose, This Patient*
- A request for an antibiotic, a scan or a referral you have decided against: *When You Say No to an Antibiotic or a Scan*
- Shouting, a threat, a request that keeps coming back, a disagreement with a colleague: *Hostile Encounters in Neutral Words*
- A patient who may lack capacity to decide, or a surrogate deciding instead: *The Capacity Question, Decision by Decision*
- A patient who declines part of the plan but stays in care: *Informed Refusal and the Partial Yes*
- A patient leaving against advice, or already gone: *The Patient Leaving Against Medical Advice*
- A discharge from emergency or urgent care; a patient who comes back: *The Discharge and the Patient Who Comes Back*
- A sign-out, a cross-cover page at night, the morning handback: *Handoffs, Cross-Cover, and the Night Call*
- A video visit, advice by phone or portal, an after-hours call: *The Telehealth Visit and the Portal Message*
- A missed visit, a patient lost to follow-up, a stalled referral: *Missed Visits and Referrals That Go Nowhere*
- An adverse event; a records request, board complaint or subpoena: *The Adverse Event and the Records Request*

## By setting

No scenario belongs to one setting alone; these are the ones that recur in each.

### Hospital medicine

The abnormal result, the bed that never opens, the consultant who never comes, hostile encounters, the capacity question, informed refusal, leaving against medical advice, handoffs and cross-cover, the adverse event.

### Emergency and urgent care

The test you order and the test you don't, the abnormal result, the delayed scan, the consultant who never comes, the prescription, hostile encounters, informed refusal, leaving against medical advice, the discharge and the patient who comes back.

### Primary care

The abnormal result, the prescription, the antibiotic or scan you decline, the telehealth visit and the portal message, missed visits and referrals that go nowhere.

## What each note must contain

### The Test You Order and the Test You Don't

- **The indication, or the reason not to test.** For a test ordered, the question it was meant to answer and the finding that raised it. For a test weighed and not ordered, the test by name and why it was not needed.
- **The decision rule and its inputs.** The rule by name, the findings that place the patient inside it, each criterion with its value, the times the rule depends on, and the output stated no larger than it is.
- **The patient discussion.** When the evidence left a real choice, the options, what each would cost, and the patient's preference in their own words.
- **The deferred plan.** For a test left for later, who orders it, when it happens, what the patient was told and what they will do if the date slips.

### Closing the Loop on the Abnormal Result

- **The result and its reviewer.** The value, when it posted or was phoned in and to whom, and when you saw it.
- **The action.** What you decided about the result, including a decision to change nothing, with its reason.
- **Who was notified.** The person you reached, how you confirmed who they were, what they were told and what they said back; when another clinician takes over, that clinician by name and their reply.
- **The method and the time.** Phone, portal, bedside or letter, each attempt with its time, failed ones included.
- **The plan if the patient cannot be reached.** The next attempt, its method and its deadline, written before you know you will need it.

### The Delayed Scan and the Bed That Never Opens

- **The delay as a fact.** What is delayed, who told you and when, the estimate and each change to it, with no comment on the department or facility.
- **The interim plan and its owner.** What you did for the condition you suspected, and who was responsible for the patient while they waited.
- **The limits that end the wait.** The findings that would make you stop waiting, who watches for them and what you will do.
- **Each reassessment.** Its time, the findings that bear on the danger and the decision, including the decision to keep waiting.
- **For a transfer.** The accepting facility and clinician, when they accepted and what it is pending, transport and its estimate, the alternatives considered, what the patient and family were told, and when you will act if the bed does not open.

### The Consultant Who Never Comes

- **The trigger.** The finding that made the consult urgent, with its time and onset.
- **Each call.** The time, the person or role, the route, and the message or question as sent.
- **Each answer.** The reply or its absence, with the time and with no adjective or guessed reason; a phone recommendation as the consultant's, read back, with whether they had seen the patient.
- **The interim care.** What you did while waiting, and each reassessment with its findings and its decision, including the decision to escalate.
- **Each escalation step.** The physician you work with and each chain-of-command contact: name, role, time, what you told or asked them, and what they did.
- **The handover.** Who took over, when, and with what plan.

### Why This Drug, This Dose, This Patient

- **The indication.** The diagnosis and expected course that call for the drug, why this drug over the alternatives, and the dose, frequency and threshold for any as-needed dose.
- **The risks discussed.** The risks for this patient in the words used, the patient's reply, and instructions on alcohol, driving, storage and disposal where they apply.
- **The checks performed.** Allergies with their reactions, interactions with what they changed, the risk factors behind the plan, and for a controlled substance the PDMP result with its time, the relevant history and any agreement your state or policy requires.
- **The duration.** The quantity, the refills and the days they cover.
- **Who reassesses and when.** The clinician and visit that decide what comes next, and the return instructions for what the drug could hide.

### When You Say No to an Antibiotic or a Scan

- **The request.** The patient's words and the concern behind them.
- **The decision.** What you did not prescribe or order, written as your clinical choice, with its date.
- **The clinical reason.** The diagnosis the request aimed at, with the findings that set it aside, and the dangerous alternative with its own.
- **The explanation and the reply.** What the patient heard about their own illness, and their answer, disagreement included.
- **What was offered instead.** Symptomatic treatment, a delayed prescription with its conditions, a referral or recheck, and what was accepted.
- **The signs that change the answer.** Those that mean start the delayed plan, and those that mean be seen today.
- **The way back.** Who reassesses and when, and how the record will learn the delayed plan was used.

### Hostile Encounters in Neutral Words

- **The report and your look.** What was raised, by whom, and your exam or review in response, with times.
- **Each request and your answer.** The request in the person's words, and the clinical reason, given once.
- **The behavior, observed.** What was said and done, without adjectives, and a threat in its exact words; the rest of any incident goes in your organization's reports.
- **What changed for the patient.** Security, visiting and updates, and the patient's own wishes.
- **The physician asked for.** Who was called, when, and what they did.
- **For a disagreement.** The option considered, the findings behind it, the decision and its owner, and any escalation; never the conflict.

### The Capacity Question, Decision by Decision

- The decision assessed, named specifically, and the time of the assessment.
- What the patient was told, and any interpreter or sensory aid used.
- Each of the four abilities, with the patient's own answers, right or wrong.
- A finding limited to this decision at this time, with its likely cause.
- Whether the decision could wait; each reassessment, timed, with what changed.
- Who assessed, and any psychiatry or ethics consultation: question, answer, action.
- For a surrogate: name, relationship, source of authority, what they were told, what they chose and why.

### Informed Refusal and the Partial Yes

- **The recommendation.** What was proposed, why, who would do it and who was present.
- **The risks in plain words.** The risks of refusing for this patient, the most serious first, and those of the test or treatment itself.
- **The alternatives.** Each option offered, including going without and what would replace it.
- **The patient's own words.** The reason for the refusal and the patient's account of what could happen.
- **The capacity finding.** For this decision at this time, with the answers behind it.
- **The partial yes.** What was accepted, what was refused and what the refusal changed in the plan.
- **The next offer.** That the patient may change their mind, who will ask again and when, and orders updated to match.
- **The form.** Signed or declined, never in place of the conversation.

### The Patient Leaving Against Medical Advice

- **The departure.** The time, the patient's reason in their own words, and the care still recommended.
- **The informed refusal.** The risks of leaving now, the alternatives offered (including help with the reason for leaving), and the capacity finding, written as the chapters on informed refusal and the capacity question describe.
- **The door left open.** The invitation to return as it was said, and the patient's reply.
- **What the patient left with.** Prescriptions accepted or declined, lines removed, instructions for this illness, pending results and their owner.
- **The follow-up.** Who sees the patient next, when, and who arranged it.
- **Who was told.** The attending and anyone else policy names, with times.
- **The form.** Signed or declined, filed beside the note and never in place of it.
- **The unwitnessed departure.** When the patient was last seen and found gone, the search, what they had been told, each attempt to reach them, and who was notified. Never a conversation that did not happen.

### The Discharge and the Patient Who Comes Back

- **The disposition and why.** Where the patient went, what made it safe enough, the options offered and the patient's choice.
- **The uncertainty carried out the door.** The working diagnosis labeled as working, and the dangers not yet excluded.
- **Precautions for this diagnosis.** Each open danger as a sign the patient can recognize, with a threshold and a place that will be open.
- **What the patient understood.** Their account in their own words, and the written copy.
- **The follow-up.** Who by name, when, how to reach them, who was told, and what happens if the appointment cannot be made.
- **On a return visit.** The earlier note reviewed, a fresh history and exam, each change with values from both visits, and the decision with its reason.

### Handoffs, Cross-Cover, and the Night Call

- **What you were told.** The source and time of the sign-out, and the items your decisions rest on.
- **The pending items.** Each item accepted, its result and what you did, or the clinician you handed it back to.
- **The call.** Who called, when, and what they reported.
- **Your own look.** When you saw the patient, the findings that bear on the problem, what you checked in the chart, and any disagreement with the sign-out, as facts.
- **Your assessment.** The working diagnosis and alternatives in your words, and whether the inherited plan still fit.
- **What you did and whom you told.** Orders with times; the physician you called, when, whether they saw the patient and what they advised.
- **The limits and the next look.** What the nurse will call you for, and when you will see the patient again.
- **The handback.** What changed overnight, what is pending, which decisions now belong to the day team, the limits still in force, and who took over and when.

### The Telehealth Visit and the Portal Message

- **The modality.** Video, phone or portal; start and end times; any switch partway, and when.
- **Who, where and consent.** How identity was confirmed, where the patient was, who else was present, and consent as your organization requires.
- **The remote exam, by source.** What you observed, what the patient's devices measured on camera, what was only reported, and what could not be assessed.
- **Why remote was enough.** The findings that made remote care reasonable, and the in-person option offered.
- **The threshold for in-person care.** The signs or readings that bring the patient in, and those that send them to the emergency department.
- **Phone and portal advice.** Who asked, in their words; what was advised and by whom; how the answer was delivered and whether it was received.
- **The after-hours call.** The time, your chart access, the advice, the escalation threshold with the patient's read-back, and who owns the next step by when.

### Missed Visits and Referrals That Go Nowhere

- **Why the follow-up matters.** At the call or visit that sets it up, the reason in plain words and what the patient said back.
- **The open item.** Where the chart will show it until it closes, such as the problem list or an alert.
- **Each missed visit, noticed.** Who reviewed it, when, and what was decided.
- **Each attempt.** Time, method, what was said or left, and the reply or its absence; a copy of any letter.
- **The next step and its date.** At the end of every entry, within the steps your policy sets.
- **The referral, tracked.** Whether it was received and scheduled, whether the report came back, who checked and when, and who owns the next step until the report has been read.
- **The patient's answer.** Their reason in their own words; a refusal written as an informed refusal, and silence never written as one.
- **Where the effort ended.** What was done, that the item is still open, and how the chart will catch the next contact.

### The Adverse Event and the Records Request

- **The event as facts.** What happened and when, with the source of anything you did not witness; no guess at how it came about, no blame.
- **The patient's condition.** Findings with times and values, not "stable."
- **What was done.** Treatment and orders, with times.
- **How long the danger lasts.** Monitoring, call limits and an end point to match.
- **Who was told.** Clinicians involved in the care, with times; never the report or the call to risk management.
- **The disclosure.** When, who was present and who spoke, what was said, the patient's questions in their words with the answers, and who speaks to them next.
- **After a request, complaint or subpoena.** No new entries about the past care and no changes; today's care charted as usual.

## Before you sign

Read the list for your scenario once the note is drafted, and look for each element in what you wrote; one you cannot find is a sentence to add before you sign, while a change is still just an edit. The lists are not a form to fill in: an element is there when your own sentences show it, not when a heading names it.
