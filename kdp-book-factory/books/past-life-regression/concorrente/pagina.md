Aanel Victoria — 4 stelle — "Interesting but flawed"
Reviewed in the United States on December 27, 2024 — Format: Kindle — Verified Purchase
This book has some interesting points that I agree with, and some insight worth
reading. The central past-life subject, who lived around the year 1300 in what
later became the UK, is interesting.

At the same time, the book has a number of flaws. It states, for instance,
"Prince Philip wasn't Andrew's father; the man who discovered King Tut's tomb
was." Howard Carter (who discovered the tomb) died in 1939; his patron George
Herbert, 5th Earl of Carnarvon, died in 1923. Prince Andrew was born in 1960.
Doesn't Sarah do even the least little bit of fact-checking on the checkable
claims in her book? Clearly the man who has been suggested as Andrew's real
father, and who was long subject to such rumors, is that Earl's grandson, Henry
Herbert, 7th Earl of Carnarvon, who was Elizabeth's horse-racing manager. And
the castle where the book asserts some of the Tut relics are hidden would be the
familial estate Highclere Castle, of 'Downton Abbey' fame.

The book also states, "Prince Andrew is not fully royal. The story of Prince
Harry was used to cover this up." This makes no sense (Andrew was born in 1960,
and Harry was born in 1984), but Sarah does not ask for any clarification.

Further items I take issue with: Contrary to statements in this book, Jesus did
not have any children; please read 'The Disappearance of the Universe' (2004) by
Gary Renard. Also, Jesus never used any "sacred objects" or power symbols; he
achieved enlightenment solely via the power of his wise thoughts and decisions;
again, please read that 2004 Gary Renard book.

There are a number of other issues I had with the book, which is a bit of a
jumble of information that often seems to promise a lot but say little. It also
was obviously rushed to print, because there are a lot of repeated formatting
errors and errors of typography (at least in the Kindle version).

All of that said, I did enjoy some of it, including the main past-life account I
mentioned at the top. I also enjoyed the information on the Reptilians, which
seems well worded and accurate.

All in all, I'd say this book is a bit of a mixed bag, and should be read with a
grain of salt or an internal filter system which separates that which seems
accurate and resonant from that which does not.
