# The Grief That Moved, and the Claim I Am Making About It

Everything up to here has been subtraction. No proof, no confirmation, no dates, no verification, no promises. This chapter is the one thing I am willing to add, and I want to state it with enough precision that you can disagree with it accurately.

It is a narrow claim. It is narrower than what this field usually offers and, I think, more defensible than anything else on the shelf.

## Three people at three months

**Gill** came eighteen months after her husband died. She was functioning — working, seeing people, doing everything she was supposed to do — and she described herself in the intake as "stuck in a way I can't explain to anybody without sounding ungrateful."

Her session contained nothing about him. It contained a long, slow, fairly dull account of being in a field and waiting for weather. She was mildly disappointed.

At three months she wrote: *I don't know what happened but I'm not carrying it in front of me any more. It's behind me, where it should be. I still think about him constantly. The difference is that I'm not braced.*

**Yusuf** came about a recurring dream he had had since he was nineteen. Same dream, perhaps twice a month, for two decades: a corridor, and a decision he could not make.

His session produced a corridor. I want to be honest that this is the least surprising outcome imaginable and prove nothing whatever — a man who has dreamt of a corridor for twenty years, invited to relax and report, produces a corridor.

At three months: *It's stopped. I keep expecting it back. My wife says I'm easier in the mornings, which I hadn't noticed.*

At a year: still stopped.

**Maureen**, whose session appears earlier in this book, stopped waking at three in the morning and stopped counting stairs.

Three people. Three self-reports. No control, no blinding, no follow-up by anybody but me, and a strong incentive on all three to tell me something good.

## What I am claiming

Exactly this: **people who do this work frequently report, afterwards, that something has changed, and those reports are worth taking seriously as reports.**

Unpack each part, because each is load-bearing.

*People who do this work.* Not everybody. A proportion. I do not know the proportion and I will come to why.

*Frequently report.* Report. They say so. This is testimony about their own lives, which is the same category as everything else in this book and has the same standing — high for the thing it describes, silent about anything beyond it.

*Something has changed.* Something. Not a symptom cured, not a condition treated. A change they noticed and considered worth mentioning.

*Worth taking seriously as reports.* Not as evidence that I did something. Not as evidence about past lives, which they say nothing about at all. Worth taking seriously in the way you take seriously a person telling you they feel better, which is how the greater part of human life is conducted.

That is the whole claim. It is about what happens after, it rests on what people say, and it makes no reference whatsoever to whether the content of any session corresponds to anything.

## What I am not claiming

*That I did it.* I do not know why Gill stopped being braced. Nothing in her session was about her husband. The honest description is that she spent an afternoon in a chair and three months later her grief sat differently, and any mechanism I offered would be an invention.

*That it was the session rather than the passage of time.* Grief moves. Dreams stop. Phobias ease. All three of my examples had been going on for a long time, which makes spontaneous change less likely but does not make it impossible, and there is no version of this in which I can rule it out.

*That anyone should expect it.* A quarter of my sessions produce nothing at all. Of those that produce something, some are followed by a reported change and many are not. A reader booking a session on the strength of this chapter would be doing something I have tried hard not to encourage.

*That it is treatment.* It is not, I am not qualified to provide it, and the session I ended early is in this book precisely because the distinction matters more than anything else in it.

*That the mechanism is anything in particular.* It might be the material. It might be an hour of undivided attention, which almost nobody gets. It might be the act of deciding to do something about a thing you have carried for twenty years, of which booking is the first step and the session is the second. I cannot distinguish these and neither can anyone else.

## Why my evidence is weak, stated plainly

My record consists of replies to the two-day note and the occasional message afterwards. That is it.

It is self-selected: people who feel something happened write back, and people who feel nothing happened mostly do not. It is unblinded in both directions. It is gathered by the person who provided the service, which is the worst possible collector. It has no denominator, because I do not know how many of the people who did not reply had no change to report and how many simply do not write letters.

I could improve this. A structured follow-up at three months to everybody, collected by somebody who is not me, with a fixed set of questions, would take a modest amount of effort and would give a number. I have not done it, for the same mixture of reasons I gave earlier about the study I did not run, and the honest element in that mixture is that a number would be a number and might not be a good one.

So when I say "frequently," I am reporting an impression formed over fourteen years by an interested party with no systematic records. You should discount it accordingly. I would.

## The ones where nothing changed

A chapter of three good outcomes is a chapter I would not trust if somebody else had written it, so here are the other kind, from the same period.

Callum still gets the smell. He came about a specific thing, had a striking afternoon, and the specific thing is unaltered at a year. His own view is that the session was worth doing anyway, for a reason that has nothing to do with the smell.

Ellen has no symptom and reports no change, because there was nothing to change. She found the afternoon remarkable and her life is exactly as it was.

Bernadette, who spent an hour being bored in an imaginary meeting, reports nothing whatsoever and finds the whole business funny.

And there is a woman I have not written about who came about a fear of flying, had a vivid, emotionally complete session that went exactly where she hoped it would, and is still unable to board an aeroplane. She wrote to tell me so, without rancour, about eight months afterwards. I have thought about her often while writing the last few pages, because her session was more convincing than Douglas's and produced nothing at all.

That last pairing is the one to hold onto. Two people, two sessions, the more compelling of the two followed by nothing and the thinner one followed by a swimming pool. If the content were doing the work, it should not come out that way round.

## Why the modest claim is the strong one

Here is the argument for making this claim and no other.

The large claim — that these sessions recover real lives — cannot be supported by anything available, as the last several chapters have set out at length. It is not that the evidence is thin. It is that the evidence required does not exist and mostly never existed, and no amount of good faith or careful practice changes that.

A field that rests its case there has built on ground that cannot bear it, and every reader who checks will find that out. That is the mechanism by which this shelf loses its readers, one at a time, and it is entirely self-inflicted.

The modest claim sits on ground that can bear it. People do report changes. That is observable, it is the ordinary currency of every talking profession, and it does not require anyone to believe anything about the seventeenth century. It is also, I think, the thing that actually matters to the people in the chair — not one of my clients has ever come to me wanting to prove a metaphysical proposition. They come because something is stuck.

And there is a practical point. A claim this narrow cannot be destroyed by a sceptical reader with a search engine, because it does not depend on any fact about the world outside the people making it. It can only be tested the way such claims are tested: by better follow-up than I have done.

## What I would want, if anyone ever does this properly

Three-month and twelve-month follow-up on every client, collected by a third party, with the same questions asked of everyone including the people whose sessions produced nothing. That last group is the control that is sitting there unused in every practice in this country.

If the people who got nothing report change at the same rate as the people who got a vivid life, that tells us the content is not what does the work, which would be the most useful finding this field could produce and would cost almost nothing to establish.

I would like to know. I think I would like to know. That hesitation is in the sentence on purpose, and it is the most honest thing in this chapter.
