# A Note on How This Book Reports Things

This is a book about what people say when they are lying in a reclining chair with their eyes closed, being asked gentle questions about a life they do not remember having lived. It is also a book about the difference between reporting that and claiming something about the world. Those two things get run together constantly on this shelf, and running them together is the reason so many readers arrive here already suspicious.

So before a single session is told, here is how the book works. It takes four minutes to read and it binds every page that follows.

## What these accounts are

The sessions in this book are written accounts, not transcripts. They are built from the shape that sessions of this kind take: the pauses in the same places, the details that arrive first, the questions that open something and the questions that close it. Where you read a stretch of speech in quotation marks, you are reading prose written to sound the way people sound in that chair, because that texture is part of what I want you to understand. You are not reading a recording of an identifiable person, and nothing in this book asks you to believe that a particular named individual said a particular sentence on a particular afternoon. The same is true of everything around the sessions — the intake conversations, the recordings and notebooks I refer to, the searches, the phone calls, the letters afterwards, and the practitioner who tells you about them, whose voice is built from the way careful practitioners of this work proceed rather than taken from one person's career — and all of it should be read the same way.

I have been as plain about that here as I know how to be, at the front, on the first pages, rather than in small type at the back. A book whose entire argument is that accounts of this work should be honest about what they are cannot begin by being cagey about what it is.

What that costs the book: you cannot treat any session here as a data point. Nobody should. What it buys the book: everything else in it can be taken at face value, which on this shelf is not nothing.

## The rule the book keeps

Every session in these pages is reported as testimony. That means the book tells you what was said and what was felt, and stops there. When something in a session touches the world outside the room — a name, a date, a place, an object, a claim about how something was built or where something was buried — the book says so, says what checking such a thing would actually involve, and says what tends to come back. Usually what comes back is nothing. The chapters that deal with that say nothing, in those words, rather than going quiet and moving on to the next story.

You will notice this most in the eight session chapters in the middle of the book. Each one ends with a short, flat note about what in it could be checked. Six of those notes report that there was nothing checkable in the session at all. One reports a search that came back empty. One reports a match, and then spends most of the chapter explaining why the match is worth less than it looks. That proportion is not modesty. It is what this work is actually like, and a book that reversed the proportion would be lying to you with true sentences.

## What is composed, and what I have held fixed

Nobody in these pages is a real, identifiable person. Names, jobs, towns, the ages of children, the make of a car, the month: all of it belongs to the composition, and any resemblance to someone you know is coincidence. People tell practitioners things in that chair they have not told their families, and nobody's afternoon has been borrowed to make this book.

What I have held fixed is the proportion. Inside each account, a check finds what a check of that kind finds, and no more. I have not placed a detail so that a search would come back full, and I have not written a search that turns up more than the records described in this book could turn up. Tilting either would turn the honest part of this book into decoration.

## What you will and will not find

You will find a complete account of what a session is, minute by minute, from the hour of conversation before anyone closes their eyes to the note I send two days afterwards. You will find eight session chapters, nine sessions in all — one chapter holds two sisters — each followed from the chair to the check, including one that I stopped early and one in which nothing happened at all. You will find the ordinary explanations for this material laid out properly rather than waved at, because a reader who has not met them cannot judge anything else in here. You will find practical chapters near the end on choosing a practitioner and preparing for a first session.

You will not find a claim that any of this proves anything about what happens after death, or before birth. You will not find promises. Where someone in these pages says they feel better afterwards, you will be told so, and told why that proves nothing about the session; nobody in it is told what is going to happen to them. You will not find another writer, book or practitioner held up as an example of how not to do it; I have views, and they stay in my own lane.

And you will not find contempt. I want to be clear about this, because "honest" and "sneering" have become tangled up in this field until people assume that anyone promising rigour is warming up to tell them they are fools. I am not. I think what happens in that chair matters, and I think it matters enough to describe accurately. The people who come to me are not credulous. They are, most of them, dealing with something — a fear they have lived with for years, a grief that will not move, a dream that has come back for most of their adult life — and they have arrived at my door having already tried the obvious things.

## A note on health

Nothing in this book is medical, psychological or therapeutic advice, and a regression session is not a treatment for anything. If you are living with a fear, a grief, pain, low mood or anything else that is affecting your life, the right first conversation is with your doctor or a qualified mental health professional. Do not stop, reduce or change any medication or treatment because of anything you read here or experience in a session. If you have a history of psychosis, dissociation or severe trauma, or you are under psychiatric care, talk to the person treating you before booking a session of any kind. If you are in crisis, the contacts are in the chapter on the weeks after a session; please do not wait.

## If you want to put the book down

Some readers want the other kind of book, and there is no shame in that. If you are looking for an account that tells you these sessions are recovered history, that the names check out, that the field has been vindicated and the sceptics routed, this is not it and it will irritate you for two hundred pages.

If, on the other hand, you have read something in this genre, looked up one of its claims, found it wrong, and quietly stopped trusting the whole shelf — this book was written with you in mind. I am asking you to stay through the sessions even where the record comes back empty, and I am promising in exchange that I will never tell you the record came back full when it did not.

That is the whole arrangement. The rest is the work.
