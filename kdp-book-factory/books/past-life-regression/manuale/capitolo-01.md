# A Note on How This Book Reports Things

This is a book about what people say when they are lying in a reclining chair with their eyes closed, being asked gentle questions about a life they do not remember having lived. It is also a book about the difference between reporting that and claiming something about the world. Those two things get run together constantly on this shelf, and running them together is the reason so many readers arrive here already suspicious.

So before a single session is told, here is how the book works. It takes four minutes to read and it binds every page that follows.

## What these accounts are

The sessions in this book are written accounts, not transcripts. They are built from the shape that sessions of this kind take: the pauses in the same places, the details that arrive first, the questions that open something and the questions that close it. Where you read a stretch of speech in quotation marks, you are reading prose written to sound the way people sound in that chair, because that texture is part of what I want you to understand. You are not reading a recording of an identifiable person, and nothing in this book asks you to believe that a particular named individual said a particular sentence on a particular afternoon.

I have been as plain about that here as I know how to be, at the front, in the first hundred words, rather than in small type at the back. A book whose entire argument is that accounts of this work should be honest about what they are cannot begin by being cagey about what it is.

What that costs the book: you cannot treat any session here as a data point. Nobody should. What it buys the book: everything else in it can be taken at face value, which on this shelf is not nothing.

## The rule the book keeps

Every session in these pages is reported as testimony. That means the book tells you what was said and what was felt, and stops there. When something in a session touches the world outside the room — a name, a date, a place, an object, a claim about how something was built or where something was buried — the book says so, says what checking such a thing would actually involve, and says what tends to come back. Usually what comes back is nothing. The chapters that deal with that say nothing, in those words, rather than going quiet and moving on to the next story.

You will notice this most in the eight full session chapters in the middle of the book. Each one ends with a short, flat note about what in it could be checked. Four of those notes report that there was nothing checkable in the session at all. One of them reports a match, and then spends three pages explaining why the match is worth less than it looks. That proportion is not modesty. It is what this work is actually like, and a book that reversed the proportion would be lying to you with true sentences.

## What I have changed, and the one thing I never change

Details have been altered throughout: names, jobs, towns, the ages of children, the make of a car, the month. Anyone who has done this work will recognise the practice and the reason for it. People tell you things in that chair they have not told their families.

The one category I never alter is the checkable one. If a session as reported contains a date, that date is as it was given. If a name is pronounced a certain way, it is written the way it sounded. I will change a client's occupation without hesitation and I will not touch the detail a reader might go and look up, because moving that detail would turn the honest part of this book into decoration.

## What you will and will not find

You will find a complete account of what a session is, minute by minute, from the hour of conversation before anyone closes their eyes to the note I send two days afterwards. You will find eight sessions told end to end, including one that I stopped early and one in which nothing happened at all. You will find the ordinary explanations for this material laid out properly rather than waved at, because a reader who has not met them cannot judge anything else in here. You will find practical chapters near the end on choosing a practitioner and preparing for a first session.

You will not find a claim that any of this proves anything about what happens after death, or before birth. You will not find promises. Nobody in this book is cured of anything, and nobody in it is told what is going to happen to them. You will not find another writer, book or practitioner held up as an example of how not to do it; I have views, and they stay in my own lane.

And you will not find contempt. I want to be clear about this, because "honest" and "sneering" have become tangled up in this field until people assume that anyone promising rigour is warming up to tell them they are fools. I am not. I think what happens in that chair matters, and I think it matters enough to describe accurately. The people who come to me are not credulous. They are, most of them, dealing with something — a fear that has outlasted every sensible treatment, a grief that will not move, a dream that has repeated for thirty years — and they have arrived at my door having already tried the obvious things.

## If you want to put the book down

Some readers want the other kind of book, and there is no shame in that. If you are looking for an account that tells you these sessions are recovered history, that the names check out, that the field has been vindicated and the sceptics routed, this is not it and it will irritate you for two hundred pages.

If, on the other hand, you have read something in this genre, looked up one of its claims, found it wrong, and quietly stopped trusting the whole shelf — this book was written with you in mind. I am asking you to stay through the sessions even where the record comes back empty, and I am promising in exchange that I will never tell you the record came back full when it did not.

That is the whole arrangement. The rest is the work.
