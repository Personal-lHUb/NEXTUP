## Segnalazioni del collegio

### [importante] fact-checker — federal rule presented as the bank's discretion
Problema: For electronic transfers (EFTA/Regulation E, 12 CFR 1005.11), money coming back during a debit dispute is not only up to the bank. If the bank does not resolve the error within 10 business days (20 for new accounts), it generally has to credit the disputed amount provisionally while it keeps investigating. It may make that credit depend on written confirmation of the dispute. The passage also leaves out that for charges you never authorized, how much you can be held responsible for depends on how quickly you report them.
Passaggio: «A disputed debit charge has already left your account, and whether and how quickly any of it comes back during the bank's review depends on that bank's process.»
Suggerimento: Rewrite along these lines: «A disputed debit charge has already left your account. Federal rules for electronic transfers generally require the bank either to finish its review quickly or to put the disputed amount back in your account provisionally while it keeps looking; it may ask you to confirm the dispute in writing first. For a charge you never authorized, how soon you report it also affects how much of it you can be held responsible for. The bank's error-resolution notice and the FTC and CFPB websites explain the timelines.» You can drop the number of days if you want to stay in line with the book's policy, but not the fact that the rule is federal.

### [minore] fact-checker — federal right attributed to the contract
Problema: What you still have to pay during a card dispute comes from federal law, not the issuer's terms. Once you send a written notice of a billing error within the deadline, you don't have to pay the disputed amount or the charges tied to it until the investigation ends, and the issuer can't report it as late. You do have to pay the rest of the bill. In the same paragraph, the notice deadlines are said to be 'set out in your cardholder or account agreement'. In fact federal law sets them (60 days from the statement, for cards and for electronic transfers alike), and the notices only summarize them.
Passaggio: «the issuer's terms explain what you still need to pay while the question is open.»
Suggerimento: Rewrite: «Under federal rules, while a card dispute filed the right way is open, you generally don't have to pay the amount in question, only the rest of the bill; the "billing rights" notice that comes with your statements explains how.» In the paragraph before, change «are set out in your cardholder or account agreement» to «are set by federal rules and summarized in the notices your card issuer and bank send you».

### [minore] lettore-cieco — lessico che cambia per strada
Problema: Nel cap. 7 imparo a distinguere le bollette fixed (stessa cifra ogni mese) dalle variable (utenze, carte). Dal cap. 11 «fixed bills» vuol dire invece «tutte le bollette mensili». Il cap. 13 manda «every fixed bill» sul conto delle bollette, e il cap. 17 deve precisare che fra le fixed entrano anche le utenze con importo variabile. Arrivato al conto di fine mese mi sono fermato a chiedermi se la bolletta della luce andasse contata o no.
Passaggio: «The second is the total of the fixed bills on your list, meaning every monthly bill, including utilities whose amount moves, using the amounts actually paid rather than the ones you expected.»
Suggerimento: Due parole diverse per due cose diverse, per esempio «regular monthly bills» per la cadenza e «fixed» solo per l'importo, usate sempre allo stesso modo.
